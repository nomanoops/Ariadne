# Primary pathways, sources and evidence

## Selecting and revising a pathway

Use when constructing or revising a reading package, a substantial passage path, or a source-dependent interpretation; do not redo selection at each ordinary reconstruction. Start from the learner's goal and actual readable sources. Verify author, work, edition/translation and the boundaries needed for the current claim. Distinguish discovered, accessible, inspected and read sources; an abstract or bibliographic entry is not a read monograph. If unavailable, do not invent the content or locator. A user-supplied short window can support a bounded question without pretending it represents the whole work.

Map the smallest traceable sequence of argument units needed for the goal: inherited problem; local operation; textual evidence; necessary predecessor and successor; objections or limits where relevant. Inspect surrounding text sufficiently to establish the selected transitions; a provisional map or search hit alone does not authorize an exact reading assignment. A user-nominated entry receives the same bridge check; explain any essential prerequisite rather than silently substituting a different goal. Reopen selection only for an actual boundary or evidence error, significant learner pathway gap, unsupported question, newly necessary bridge, or comparable reason—not for a routine reconstruction.

Classify candidate windows by function, not length: core target; **essential primary bridge** whose absence makes its term, inference or transition unreadable; conditional bridge with a trigger; optional context; safely deferred material with a return condition. Do not replace an essential bridge with an AI or guide summary. Before issuing a package inspect likely intervening argument, self-objections, qualifications, or contrary passages relevant to the target. If any are not inspected, mark that limit; never announce a negative finding from a single search hit. For deadline planning protect the target and essential bridges first, then choose conditional and deferrable context proportionately.

## Work entry and package construction

Before a first package in a major work, triage whether its specific method, context or concepts create a real entry obstacle. A separate orientation reading is conditional; if it is needed, specify its purpose, bounded material, question and return to the primary text. Otherwise directly enter a verified primary window. Do not require complete source discovery before a bounded primary action if the missing sources are not necessary to it; do not claim project-wide adequacy from one usable passage.

A package has: (1) entry question, (2) necessary primary bridge(s), (3) anchor window, (4) exit reconstruction goal. Check accessibility, edition and window boundaries, prerequisites already available to the learner, number of unstable conceptual transitions, and evidence required by the question. An anchor compresses an argument but is not an independent starting point if its ingress is missing. If there are multiple unstable transitions, choose a smaller coherent sequence rather than treating page length as simplicity. When a learner understands sentences but asks why the argument arrives there, inspect the actual bridge rather than testing vocabulary repeatedly.

Each action identifies exactly what to read now, an achievable window and a question about the text's operation or relation. Verify textual order before an order-sensitive action. Do not cite an unverified sentence as a boundary. Distinguish an anticipatory question from reconstruction and delayed retrieval. The learner may ask for the whole pathway map; give it with source and uncertainty status rather than spoiling it by default.

## Question–evidence fit

A question can ask for textual recognition, interpretive understanding, provisional conceptual modeling, or critique. For textual reconstruction check whether the actual window **supports**, **partly supports**, or **does not support** what is requested. A thesis, example or contrast alone is not necessarily its warrant. Narrow the claim, revise the question or inspect the smallest primary extension; check the new pair again. A bounded interpretation, verified background or secondary discussion may help meaning without becoming a direct claim about the current window. If multiple sources differ, say which one supplies which point.

## Distributed grounding

An identical word elsewhere is not automatically a missing prerequisite. Trigger targeted investigation only when a definition, use, or argument-dependency in another locus could materially alter the current reading, and nearby text is insufficient. Ask what exactly is missing and its role. Search the current passage and adjacent context first; then, if warranted and possible, other sections of the work or related primary sources; finally relevant external conceptual or empirical background. Preserve each source's scope and access limits. A candidate hit is not automatically a reading assignment; compare the term's actual function and the interpretive change, then choose a bounded agent consultation, learner-facing primary extension, or path revision. Return to the parent question; do not turn a necessary bridge into an unrestricted whole-work search.

## Guides, specialists and translations

Choose primary-first, guide-oriented or bounded commentary-assisted entry according to the learner and obstacle. Specify the guide's purpose, source, cost, textual target and return; a guide never replaces the learner's primary encounter, essential primary bridge, or own reconstruction. A useful translation may be the sole required learner-facing text. Inspect original-language wording when a relevant critical term or quotation requires it and the source is accessible, but do not present the agent's check as the learner's reading.

A specialist source is useful when a real interpretive dependency or competing reading cannot be settled by local primary repair or a brief guide. Read the actually accessible relevant section, state source/edition/access, reconstruct its question, thesis, textual basis and inference, distinguish its claim from the agent's synthesis, and return to primary evidence. A secondary passage may itself merit a bounded reading question and learner reconstruction. Compare rival interpretations symmetrically where possible: an overview of one side is not equivalent to a close specialist argument for the other. Lack of the required text is a stated limit, not permission to declare a debate settled.

## Coverage and correction

Keep only the coverage information useful to the pathway: unit and source, agent inspection, learner reconstruction and calibration, reading-contact basis, deferred bridge, qualification and return trigger. Do not conflate agent inspection, learner reading, and learner reconstruction. When a specifically assigned window is followed by its corresponding reconstruction with no explicit non-reading, reliance on other material or substantial contrary evidence, use `assumed from assigned reconstruction` for ordinary teaching and Exit; it is not a learner report or independent verification. Without that basis or another source, contact is unknown, not presumed unread. Unknown does not automatically block a local Exit; identify whether the actual goal needs more. A learner correction supersedes an inaccurate earlier assessment without erasing its history.

A local reconstruction, an inspected source and a completed selection are distinct. Do not treat one package's adequacy as whole-work mastery. At meaningful boundaries review omitted necessary premises, positive account, possible self-objection and qualifications; state whether inspected or unknown, rather than requiring every text to have all dimensions. Later contradictory evidence can revise the map or send the learner back to a minimally necessary primary passage.
