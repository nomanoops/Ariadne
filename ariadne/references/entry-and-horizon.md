# Entry, prior knowledge and horizon

## Purpose and trigger

Use for a sustained exploration, a change in familiarity, a learner's existing knowledge, an explicit starting question, or a substantive change in horizon. It is not a prerequisite for ordinary teaching. Keep learning exploratory: do not convert orientation into thesis production, claim an agent-generated map is the learner's view, or manufacture a research question.

## Reconstruct a starting point

Begin with what the learner has already said, supplied or authorized. Where an accessible note store is explicitly configured or offered, use it read-only within permission; otherwise skip retrieval without treating its absence as a teaching failure. Distinguish a substantive statement in a note from repeated exposure, and both from a title, tag or search hit. None proves current mastery or that a philosophical claim is correct. Identify source, scope and uncertainty; do not disclose unrelated personal content. Ask only a short retrieval-first question when whether a particular idea is currently available would change the entry, then refresh as needed instead of teaching everything from zero. Do not re-ask an answered question merely because a session changed.

Offer, not impose, an entry recommendation:
- **Orientation**: limited familiarity; begin with readable primary anchors and, if useful, reliable orientation; do not demand a mature question.
- **Domain**: broad familiarity but little structure in the present area; locate the current conceptual and textual relationships and primary entry.
- **Problem**: substantial pertinent familiarity and a learner's focused question; elicit the learner's existing horizon rather than substituting a map.

An explicitly stated long-term learning intention may call for a durable record if the host supports one and the user wants it; intent does not itself authorize a file write or require a project before teaching. Describe a suggested setup and ask only for actual permission and necessary missing details. If no persistence exists, continue in-session and offer a handoff when useful.

## H0: elicitation before challenge

When a starting horizon will actually guide a problem-oriented exploration, conduct an open interview from the learner's own words. Ask one or a small number of questions that invite their understanding, the origin and scope of their question, what seems puzzling and what they expect; follow their answer rather than impose a standard questionnaire. Do not offer menu choices of theoretical positions, introduce mature explanatory frameworks, pre-generate assumptions, or present inferred assumptions as the learner's own. If they do not know, retain the uncertainty and move toward reading rather than force an answer.

Prepare a concise snapshot separating (1) actual expressed content, close to the learner's wording, from (2) agent-inferred, unconfirmed assumptions or questions. Do not smuggle paraphrase into a quotation. Ask for correction and confirmation. Only after confirmation, and when helpful, introduce a challenge, missing perspective, rival framing or text that might alter the horizon; distinguish the agent's contribution from the learner's position. If the learner requests an alternative immediately, respond, but do not present it as already adopted.

## Work orientation and maps

Triage a new major work before its first formal package: assess whether its problem, method, background or prerequisites would make this precise entry misleading. Choose direct entry when no material obstacle is apparent, or a small orientation reading when necessary. State what this is expected to clarify and where to return. Orientation is not an automatic course or substitute for the primary pathway. Preserve a near-term deadline and name any safely deferred context and trigger.

Use only the maps the task needs: text sequence and locations; concept relations and unresolved hinges; historical or methodological context; actual disagreement and sources. Mark provisional maps, source access, counter-evidence, omitted bridges and learner contributions. Maps should preserve development, tension, qualifications and uncertainty rather than reduce a work to disconnected cards or a list of doctrines. Do not create an interpretive camp from an unnamed contrast.

## Revision and stopping

New primary evidence or a credible competing interpretation can expose an inaccurate premise, a learner-framed question absent from the text, or a transition requiring backtracking. State what changed, its basis, and a proposed local next step. The agent may adjust local teaching questions but asks the learner before changing their primary object, major scope, research question, thesis or significant interpretive commitment. Suggested questions and inferred assumptions remain scaffolds until explicitly accepted. Further possible reading alone is not a reason to extend without limit; stop the present action when it served its goal, then determine whether another action or actual human decision is needed.
