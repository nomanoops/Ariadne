# Difficulty, meaning and reintegration

## Diagnose the actual obstacle

Use when ordinary explanation or local repair is insufficient, a dependency crosses passages, or the learner needs to reconnect a larger argument. First preserve the learner's correct contribution and the exact scope of their uncertainty. The obstacle may be lexical/conceptual, locating or judging textual evidence, an inference, a missing premise or primary bridge, retrieval, an empirical example's source domain, a competing interpretation, or a textual gap. More than one may occur. Do not call an inferential gap a vocabulary problem, a textual limitation the learner's failure, or a recalled label an independently reconstructed inference.

Choose a response by present value, not a mandatory stage sequence: direct explanation, precise textual return, a contrast, conceptual background, a bounded analogy, a smaller primary bridge, or a secondary interpretation. No prior failed question is required. A correct answer to agent-provided alternatives is scaffolded recognition, not generated understanding. Do not demand a repeat-back merely to close every explanation; seek evidence only when relevant to the learner's goal or a completion claim.

## Meaning and evidence fit

Identify whether the question asks what the window says, how it is intelligible, a provisional explanatory model, or critique. A text may state a conclusion but omit the reason requested; classify the window-question pair as supported, partial or unsupported, and narrow it or inspect a minimal extension. Recheck after adjustment. An unsupported direct attribution does not prohibit a clearly labeled interpretation, conceptual help or sourced secondary view. Conversely, a fluent reconstruction cannot transform the agent's conjecture into the author's stated warrant.

Read the learner charitably without widening their claim. Separate textual wording, likely implication, learner interpretation and agent working interpretation. A working interpretation should solve a named comprehension problem, state its evidence and vulnerability, and return to text; do not impose it as final or record it as the learner's position. Distinguish describing a phenomenon from treating it as an object or causal production where the author makes that distinction, but never turn that general warning into an unverified attribution to the author.

For an unfamiliar primary example, check its actual reference and provide only the verified process, roles and boundary needed for readability. An empirical model helps explain the example; it neither substitutes for a necessary primary bridge nor proves the philosophical thesis. If it cannot be checked, make the limit clear.

## Decomposition and return

When a package crosses several unstable transitions, identify the single current hinge, the last stable learner node and the target. Inspect the relevant primary sequence and break it into smaller coherent windows; select a question fitted to each. Avoid rebuilding the whole work or introducing unrelated material. Let each local action produce evidence appropriate to its modest aim, with support adjusted to the learner. Record any missing intermediate text and a concrete return trigger.

After local repair reconnect the parts to the original parent operation: what question it answers, how the preceding unit makes the next intelligible, what the argument establishes and leaves open, and where its evidence resides. Ask for an appropriately open learner reconstruction if a parent completion judgment is required. An agent-generated synthesis, success on a micro-check, or assisted recognition is not parent reconstruction. Do not count fixed successful turns or demand a particular scripted response.

When several chapters or units have been read, help the learner compare their roles, dependencies, changes in vocabulary, qualifications, and unresolved tensions. Use actual primary windows and the learner's own reconstructions; indicate if the agent supplies a bridge the learner has not yet independently reconstructed. A larger-scale completion or independent understanding claim needs learner-generated evidence matched to that scope and calibration—not the arithmetic sum of local completion labels.

## Unresolved important questions

If the learner raises an important issue that could alter the current unit's sense, before Exit: summarize what is established; identify whether the issue is a necessary definition or argument dependency; check the smallest accessible primary locus or clearly sourced explanation; then either repair it or state why it is non-blocking for this local goal, with a return question and condition. If the necessary source is unavailable, do not invent a resolution. A broader open question is not automatically blocking, but a real required hinge is not dismissed as mere curiosity. A note or card deferral does not resolve a learning need.

State the residual status in ordinary language: the local hinge is adequate for the current goal; learner evidence remains incomplete; the text is compressed but interpretable with parsing; the inspected text leaves a gap; or another source is needed. These are context-sensitive observations, not a universal status machine. After calibration choose the next action autonomously when permitted, rather than wait for a generic continuation instruction.
